Why NOT framing this as export

  • Latent demand trap: Nigeria adds ~5M people/yr; each eats ~35kg rice. That's +175kt demand/yr just from population growth
  • Yield at 2.0 t/ha — even doubling yield to 4.0 t/ha on current area = ~10M paddy = ~6.5M milled. Still short of 9M demand
  • Export ban is de facto permanent — no government will allow rice exports while imports continue
  • ✓ The honest case is $800M/yr saved
  • ✓ Every tonne substituted = FX preserved
  • ✓ Jobs created domestically
  • ✓ DFIs understand this framing
  • ✓ Government alignment: import substitution ≠ export permission battle

Site-visit / validation questions

A. Production reality

  • Actual paddy production? ~5M or ~8M MT?
  • Insecurity impact? Which basins lost?
  • Irrigated vs rainfed share?
  • Fertiliser actual cost/ha?
  • Seed quality? How many seasons recycled?

B. Milling economics

  • Recovery rate? Actual vs claimed?
  • Energy cost/t milled?
  • Utilisation rate? Why idle?
  • Paddy procurement cost?
  • Can compete vs import at what price?

C. Import ban mechanics

  • Benin flow volume? Real estimate?
  • Consumer price premium? Local vs import?
  • Ban permanence? Any review planned?
  • ECOWAS tariff structure?
  • Quality perception: local vs import?

D. Investment appetite

  • CBN Anchor Borrowers: why mixed?
  • Private mill investment pipeline?
  • DFI activity in rice? IFC, AfDB?
  • FX risk: Naira for paddy, USD for parts?
  • State-level champions — who?
REFRAME OR ABANDON IF 2+ TRUE:
  • Insecurity expands to additional major rice basins (Benue, Niger, Kebbi)
  • Paddy production falls below 4MMT (irrecoverable without security)
  • Consumer quality gap cannot close — local rice rejected at import-competitive price
  • Import ban lifted without transition plan — flooding destroys local mills
  • FX crisis makes imported inputs (machinery, seed) unaffordable

⚠ Mali was assessed and EXCLUDED

  • ECOWAS exit — trade integration broken
  • Military government — political instability
  • Yield 1.8 t/ha — below Nigeria
  • Production exists (Office du Niger) but corridor logic fails without ECOWAS
